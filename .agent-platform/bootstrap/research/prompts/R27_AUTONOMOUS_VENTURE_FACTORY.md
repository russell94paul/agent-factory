# R27 — Low-Human Autonomous Software Venture Factory

## Objective

Determine how far a governed Agent Factory can realistically automate the lifecycle of small subscription/software businesses: research, validation, product design, implementation, testing, launch, operations, support synthesis, product learning and iterative improvement.

Do not assume "zero human" is optimal. Find the minimum human authority needed at each risk level.

## Questions

- What end-to-end agentic software-business systems have actually been demonstrated?
- Which business stages remain bottlenecked by human judgment, trust, relationships, legal authority or customer interaction?
- How should opportunity → validation → MVP → launch → operate → improve be represented as a dynamic mission graph?
- Which stages should be deterministic, agentic, externally integrated or human-gated?
- How can product telemetry and customer feedback become evidence for future agent decisions?
- How should budget, deployment, secrets, payments, customer data and public communications be permissioned?
- What metrics distinguish "software shipped" from "business validated"?
- What classes of low-risk software businesses are especially compatible with high automation?
- What causes autonomous venture loops to waste money or build unwanted products?
- How can the Factory kill weak ideas early?

## Output

- autonomy-by-business-stage matrix;
- minimum viable Venture Compiler architecture;
- commercial GreenContract proposal;
- human/policy gate matrix;
- first three safe pilot scenarios;
- integration requirements;
- experiment backlog;
- architecture changes to the bootstrap roadmap.

Attack the premise. Include reasons the autonomous-venture approach may underperform a small human-led team.
